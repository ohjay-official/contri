How it works.

The collector is replaced by a program that can only do what the circle agreed to. Each card is one step of a circle: tap or drag to turn them.

The contract holds the pot

Each circle lives in Contri's contract on NEAR. When a circle is created, its rules are fixed: the amount, the number of members, how often, and how turns are ordered. Members join by paying a deposit of one contribution. Once every seat is filled, rounds begin.

Each round, every member pays one contribution, or pays every round left at once: the contract keeps the rounds paid ahead and puts one into each new round as it starts, so a member who paid ahead never has to remember again. When the last contribution arrives, the contract sends the whole pot to the member whose turn it is, in the same transaction; a round that every member paid ahead opens already paid, and the keeper sends its pot on its next run, within minutes. If the deadline passes first, anyone can ask the contract to settle the round: missed contributions come out of the late members' deposits, then out of any backing the circle has, and the pot pays in full while those last. When the circle ends, what's left of each deposit goes back to its owner.

A deposit is one contribution, so it covers one missed round. What a member's own deposit doesn't cover, they owe: when their turn comes, that much is held back from their pot and goes to the members whose pots were short. If a member takes an early pot and then stops paying, there is nothing left to hold back: their deposit covers the first round they miss, backing covers what it can after that, and the members paid later get a smaller pot. That is the same risk as in any àjọ, which is why circles work best among people who trust each other.

Turn order is either the order people joined, with the organizer first, or drawn at random by the contract from NEAR's block randomness once the circle is full. The draw is best effort: no member or organizer can pick it, but NEAR's block producers could sway it.

Money moves through NEAR Intents

Contributions are USDT held in NEAR Intents, NEAR's network for moving and swapping assets across chains. Members top up from an exchange or wallet on BNB Chain, Solana, Tron, TON, Base, Arbitrum or Ethereum through 1Click, which turns the deposit into USDT in their account. Cashing out works the same way in reverse, to any of those networks.

A payment to a circle is an intent: a short message, signed on the member's phone, that says "move 5 USDT to Contri for circle 3, round 2". NEAR Intents checks the signature and hands the money and the message to the contract, which either accepts it or refunds it with the reason.

Your keys come from your passkey

Contri has no accounts or passwords. When you sign in, your passkey gives the app a secret that only that passkey can produce on this site (the WebAuthn PRF extension). The app turns it into one key per circle. Each key's account is a fresh NEAR account that has never been seen before, and money reaches it through NEAR Intents' confidential route, so your circle accounts aren't linked by keys on chain. Amounts and timing can still hint at a link, and when you move money between your private balance and a circle, 1Click and Contri's server see both accounts. Passkeys only work on contri.ng, so write down the recovery code too: if the site ever goes away or changes hands, don't use your passkey there, and restore your keys from the code in the open-source app. On a new phone, the same passkey gives the same keys back.

Contri pays the transaction fees

Members never need NEAR tokens. Contri's relayer takes your signed intent, checks it is a Contri payment, simulates it, and submits it, paying the NEAR fee. Contri pays for it from its share of a 0.25% fee on topping up from another network and cashing out to one, which Contri and 1Click share; everything inside a circle is free. It can't change what you signed. Contri's code keeps no logs of it; Vercel, which hosts the relayer, records each request; on our plan we can see those records for an hour. A keeper settles rounds whose deadline has passed and returns deposits after a circle ends. Anyone could do the same: the contract gives the keeper no special rights.

Turns are tradable

Each seat is an NFT that shows its turn and status, drawn by the contract itself. A member who can wait lists their turn for a price; a member with a later turn who needs the money sooner buys it, and the contract swaps the two seats and pays the seller.

Check it yourself

  • The source code for the contract, the app and the relayer.
  • The contract's own tests, and end-to-end tests that run whole circles against the real NEAR Intents contract on a local NEAR network, including the app's own code.
  • The contract's code is public, and the code running on NEAR is byte for byte what the project's CI builds from it, so anyone can check what it does.
  • The contract's account keys, on a NEAR explorer. It has none: we locked it on 7 November 2026, so nobody, us included, can change its code or move the money it holds outside its rules. NEAR Intents' governance and Tether keep their usual powers over USDT held there, as for anyone.
